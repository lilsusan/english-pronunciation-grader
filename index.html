請按照課堂指示完成錄音，並繳交檔案於 Google Classroom

1. These unprocessed silicon wafers are sometimes referred to as "blank checks" because they have no patterns before processing.
2. Taiwan's TSMC is a globally recognized foundry specializing in processing these silicon wafers into high-performance, marketable chips.
3. After the circuits are etched onto it, the silicon wafer can be sliced into dozens or even hundreds of individual units, known as "dies."
4. Once these dies undergo packaging and testing, they are transformed into the familiar black, rectangular chips we see in everyday products.
5. Wafer processing is not only complex but also requires high precision and strict quality control, as each step is crucial to the performance and quality of the final chips.
6. A chip, formally known as an integrated circuit (IC), is a semiconductor component that has been fully processed and manufactured.
7. Inside a chip, there are integrated circuits and billions of tiny components, such as transistors, resistors, and capacitors, which allow it to perform specific functions like signal processing, logical operations, and data storage.
8. Chips are widely used in various electronic products, including smartphones, household appliances, vehicles, and medical devices.
